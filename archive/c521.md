# UX Analysis & Suggested Improvements: Sober App (c521)

This document analyzes the Sober codebase from how a real user **operates** the app day to day and **experiences** outcomes (clarity, motivation, trust after a slip, paywall friction). It reflects the codebase as of May 2026. No code was changed.

Many items from the earlier `g521.md` analysis have since been addressed (celebration overlay hiding, historical timeline trees, timeline check-in editing, lifetime savings, persisted achievements, start-date editing, journal free-write/shuffle, expanded free health benefits). This document focuses on **remaining gaps** and **new** UX opportunities.

---

## 1. Home check-in is still a single tap with no reflection

**User experience & operation context:**  
The primary daily action is on Home: tap “Check in for today.” For someone building self-awareness, that moment is when they are most willing to log mood or a one-line note. Today it immediately marks the day sober and waters the garden—fast, but shallow. Mood and notes only appear if they later open Timeline, select today, and scroll to the check-in section. Most users will never do that on a good day.

**Code limitation:**  
`HomeView` calls `CheckInService.checkIn()` with no `mood` or `note` (`Sober/Features/Today/HomeView.swift`). The service and model already support those fields (`CheckInService`, `DailyCheckIn`).

**Suggested improvement:**  
After tapping check-in (or instead of instant completion), show a lightweight bottom sheet: optional 5-icon mood row (same symbols as Timeline) + optional note field + “Save” / “Skip.” Keep a “Quick check-in” path for users who want one tap. Align copy with Timeline so the two surfaces feel like one habit, not two systems.

---

## 2. Vitality and “watering” promise no visible consequence

**User experience & operation context:**  
Copy and notifications say checking in “waters your garden.” Users expect the bonsai and items to look tired if they skip days, then perk up when they return. Opening the app after a long absence should feel like coming back to something that missed them—not an always-perfect garden at 100% vitality.

**Code limitation:**  
`GardenState.vitality` and `lastWateredAt` exist; `GardenService.water()` increases vitality. Nothing in the app **decreases** vitality over missed days. Renderers already scale saturation from `vitality` (`BonsaiRenderer`, `GardenItemRenderer`), but the value never drops below full unless manually reset on journey restart.

**Suggested improvement:**  
On app foreground or during `refreshCheckInState`, compute days since `lastWateredAt` (or last sober check-in) and apply decay (e.g. −0.1 per day, floor 0.3). Pair with subtle visual wilt and recovery animation on check-in. Optionally surface “Your garden missed you” on the welcome-back card when `daysMissed > 1`.

---

## 3. Two different “slip” stories confuse trust

**User experience & operation context:**  
A user who drank yesterday might tap “I slipped” on Home (resets counter and journey) or go to Timeline and “Log a slip” for that day (red cell, counter **unchanged**). They can also use the ⋯ menu “Reset counter” with different copy than the slip alert. These paths imply different realities: calendar says slip, Home still shows 45 days sober. That erodes trust in the number on the spine.

**Code limitation:**  
`SobrietyService.reset()` / journey end only runs from Home alerts and menu (`HomeView`). Timeline `logCheckIn(wasSober: false)` only updates `DailyCheckIn` (`TimelineView`).

**Suggested improvement:**  
Unify semantics with explicit user choice:
- **“Log a slip (keep history)”** — calendar + note only; optional prompt: “Reset day counter too?”
- **“Start a new journey”** — ends active journey, resets counter (current Home behavior).

Use the same language in Home, Timeline, and Settings. When logging a slip for **today**, default to offering journey reset so counter and calendar stay aligned.

---

## 4. Day counter vs check-in calendar can disagree

**User experience & operation context:**  
The big number on Home comes from **journey start date** (`SobrietyService.daysSinceStart`). Green/red calendar cells come from **daily check-ins**. A user can have 30 “days sober” on the counter while many calendar days are empty (never checked in), or backfill sober days without the counter changing. They may think the app is “lying” when Timeline stats (Current / Longest / Total) don’t match how they mentally count sobriety.

**Code limitation:**  
Counter: `activeJourney.startDate`. Calendar: `DailyCheckIn` records. `backfillSoberDays()` fills gaps but does not adjust journey start.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Pick a single source of truth for the hero number, or explain the split in UI:
- **Option A:** Counter = consecutive sober check-ins ending today (habit-first).
- **Option B:** Counter = journey days, but auto-create sober check-ins for each day in the active journey range (calendar matches counter).

Add a one-line footer under the counter: “Based on your start date” vs “Based on daily logs,” and surface mismatches gently (“12 days on your counter · 8 days logged”).

## 5. Unlock celebrations vs what free users can actually use

**User experience & operation context:**  
Hitting Day 14 triggers a full-screen “New Unlock!” (e.g. Stone Lantern). Free users feel rewarded, return to Home—and the lantern may not appear in the garden (placement is Pro-gated; only `moss` auto-places via `freeToPlaceIDs`). The celebration trains “you earned this,” but the garden spine may look unchanged. That feels like a bait-and-switch unless Pro is clearly part of the reward.

**Code limitation:**  
`checkForUnlocks` celebrates all new items; auto-place only for `GardenItemCatalog.freeToPlaceIDs` (`HomeView`). Pro placement rules in `GardenItemCatalog.canPlace`.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Tailor celebration copy by entitlement: “Unlocked — place it in your garden with Bloom+” vs “Placed in your garden!” Preview the item on the celebration card even if not placed. For free users, show a dimmed garden preview with the new item ghosted in. Consider auto-placing one decoration tier for free (e.g. first decoration per cycle) so the emotional payoff is visible.

## 6. Earned achievements stay visually locked without Bloom+

**User experience & operation context:**  
After a relapse, users need proof past effort mattered. `UnlockedAchievement` persistence helps technically, but in Progress, earned badges still show a lock icon and grey treatment unless `isPro` (`ProgressSheet.achievementRow`). A user who earned “Perfect Week” before subscribing sees a lock—indistinguishable from never earning it. That punishes loyalty and obscures the trophy case value prop.

**Code limitation:**  
`visible = unlocked && isPro`; tap opens paywall when `!isPro`.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Show earned achievements with full color and checkmark for everyone; gate **detail** or **share** behind Pro, or show locked only for never-earned items. Copy: “You earned this on [date]. Unlock Bloom+ to see all streak badges.”

---

## 7. Progress, collection, and customize are hard to discover

**User experience & operation context:**  
The emotional core is the full-bleed garden on Home. Users looking for “what’s next,” collection, money saved, or achievements must find:
- Chart icon → Progress sheet (medium/large detent)
- ⋯ menu → Customize garden / Settings
- No tab for collection or stats

New users after onboarding land on Home with one CTA; they may never open Progress before churning.

**Code limitation:**  
`MainTabView` has Home, Timeline, Health, Journal only (`Sober/App.swift`). `GardenCollectionView` is embedded inside `ProgressSheet`, not a tab.

**Suggested improvement:**  
- Add a visible “Progress” or “Collection” chip on Home (below counter or on first launch coach mark).
- After first unlock, deep-link open Progress sheet once.
- Consider a fifth tab or Health header link: “Your garden · 8/14 items” → collection.
- Rename tab “Home” → “Garden” or “Today” to match mental model in docs and TestFlight feedback.

---

## 8. Pannable garden has no affordance for exploration

**User experience & operation context:**  
The garden world is larger than the viewport (`PannableGardenView`: drag + pinch). Users on a phone may think the scene is static wallpaper and never discover they can pan to see placed items, grove trees, or corners. Exploration is part of the delight loop.

**Code limitation:**  
Gestures exist but no onboarding hint, edge fade, or “drag to explore” label.

**Suggested improvement:**  
First-session overlay: subtle hand animation + “Drag to explore your garden.” Optional parallax on device tilt. When new items place, briefly auto-pan to the item. Haptic on snap-to-center.

---

## 9. Timeline check-in editor: mood missing on first log

**User experience & operation context:**  
On Timeline, selecting an empty day shows “Log as sober” / “Log a slip” immediately. Mood picker only appears **after** a check-in exists (`checkInEditor` branches on `checkInsByDay[day]`). Users logging yesterday retroactively get no prompt to capture how that day felt unless they log first, then edit.

**Code limitation:**  
`logCheckIn` does not pass mood; mood UI is in the `if let checkIn` branch only (`TimelineView`).

**Suggested improvement:**  
When logging sober, open the same micro-sheet as Home (mood + note) before save. Or show mood row inline on the “Log as sober” path before confirming.

---

## 10. Journal: read-only list, no entry detail or edit

**User experience & operation context:**  
Users write vulnerable entries, then see a 4-line preview in a list. They cannot tap to read full text, edit a typo, or delete something written in distress. That blocks the “look back at growth” promise on the paywall.

**Code limitation:**  
`EntryRow` displays `lineLimit(4)` with no navigation (`JournalView`). No delete API in UI.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Tap row → detail sheet with full text, feeling, date, prompt used. Swipe-to-delete or edit in toolbar. Empty state CTA should mirror Pro gate clearly: “Preview prompts free · writing requires Bloom+.”

---

## 11. Free Journal tease without a try-on

**User experience & operation context:**  
Free users see “Prompt of the day” and “Write entry,” but the pencil opens the paywall. Repeated exposure without one completed entry can feel like a locked door rather than a sample. Users cannot judge if journaling fits their recovery style before subscribing.

**Code limitation:**  
Compose gated on `subscriptions.isProSubscriber` for toolbar and prompt row actions.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Allow **one** free entry (or 3-day trial journal) stored locally, then paywall. Or show compose read-only with blurred save until upgrade. Align with Health’s “taste early wins” strategy.

---

## 12. Notifications are generic and don’t deep-link

**User experience & operation context:**  
At 2 PM the user gets “Daily check-in — Mark today sober and water your garden.” They tap, app opens to last tab—not necessarily Home—and they still hunt for the button. No personalization (day count, next milestone, “you’re on a 12-day streak”).

**Code limitation:**  
`NotificationService` static title/body; no `userInfo` URL or category actions.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Rotate copy with `currentStreakDays` from widget snapshot. Add notification category with “Check in now” action (App Intent). Deep-link to Home tab. If notifications disabled in Settings, don’t schedule silently—reflect state in onboarding.

---

## 13. Widget and Watch are view-only extensions

**User experience & operation context:**  
Users who live on Lock Screen / Apple Watch want the fastest path: see count, tap once to log. Today they must open the iPhone app and hit check-in. Watch loads snapshot on appear only—no refresh, no action.

**Code limitation:**  
`SoberWidgets` is `StaticConfiguration` only. `WatchRootView` displays `WidgetSnapshotStore.load()` with no `WidgetCenter.reload` hook or App Intents.

**Suggested improvement:**  
iOS 17+ interactive widget: “Checked in ✓” / “Check in” via App Intent writing through App Group. Watch: same intent + complication. Reload timeline after check-in in main app (`WidgetSnapshotPump` already exists).

---

## 14. Paywall hero still streak-only; trial nudge is easy to miss

**User experience & operation context:**  
`PaywallView` hero uses **current streak** savings (`days * costPerDay`), not **lifetime** savings already computed in Progress. After a reset, paywall shows $0 saved—undermining “you’ve already invested in yourself.” Trial nudge lives inside Progress sheet at day 7+; users who never open Progress never see it.

**Code limitation:**  
`PaywallView.moneySaved` uses active journey days only. `showTrialNudge` in `ProgressSheet` only.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Paywall hero: “You’ve saved $X lifetime” when `lifetimeSoberDays > 0`. Surface trial on Home once (non-blocking banner) after day 7. Post-onboarding optional paywall skip with “Maybe later.”

---

## 15. Health tab: time-unlocked vs Pro-blurred states blur together

**User experience & operation context:**  
In Health, a benefit can be (a) not yet reached in time, (b) reached but Bloom+-gated, or (c) fully visible. Free users see crown/blur on benefits they’ve **earned** by hours sober—feels like paywall blocking health facts they qualified for. “Next up” row uses lock icon even when the blocker is subscription, not time.

**Code limitation:**  
`BenefitRow` `blurred` = unlocked by hours but `idx >= freeRevealCount` and not Pro (`HealthView`).

**Suggested improvement:**  
Three distinct visual states with labels: “In X days,” “Unlocked — Bloom+,” “Unlocked.” Consider showing summary (not full NIAAA detail) free for all time-earned benefits; detail + sources for Pro. Match paywall promise: “first 2 free” → user understands what’s time vs paid.

---

## 16. Onboarding doesn’t set expectations for free vs Bloom+

**User experience & operation context:**  
Onboarding is warm and focused: start date/time, spend, reminder. Users aren’t told that journal, full health sources, customization, and achievement visibility are paid. First paywall hit may feel sudden on Journal day 3 or Health day 4.

**Code limitation:**  
`OnboardingView` has no feature map step; goes straight to `hasCompletedOnboarding = true`.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Final step: simple two-column “Free forever” vs “Bloom+” (counter, garden growth, calendar, 5 health benefits vs journal, customize, full health, stats). Sets honest expectations before emotional investment.

---

## 17. `gardenThemeID` and unused theme promise

**User experience & operation context:**  
Power users expect “zen / forest / seasonal” themes when they see garden customization marketing. Nothing in Settings or Customize changes theme; the field is inert.

**Code limitation:**  
`GardenState.gardenThemeID` stored but never read in `GardenSceneView` (sky/ground are fixed).

**Suggested improvement:**  
Either hide until implemented or ship one alternate sky/ground preset as a late milestone unlock. Avoid storing user-facing prefs that do nothing.

---

## 18. Visual fidelity limits emotional connection

**User experience & operation context:**  
Recovery apps compete on **feeling** cared for. Hand-drawn SwiftUI primitives work but read as prototype (`design-handoff/CURRENT-STATE.md`). Unlock moments, Health, and onboarding lack illustration polish users expect from wellness apps.

**Code limitation:**  
Asset pipeline for bonsai stages, items, paywall hero, onboarding art not integrated—SF Symbols and shapes throughout.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Prioritize art handoff items that touch daily loops: bonsai stages (Home spine), one celebration illustration, Health benefit icons, empty states for Journal/Timeline. UX win is emotional, not architectural.

---

## 19. Celebration overlay timing and backdrop

**User experience & operation context:**  
Users want to dismiss wins quickly or re-read them. `UnlockCelebrationView` disables Continue for **1.5 seconds** (`dismissable` delay). Backdrop is still thin material + 55% black—better than before (counter hidden on Home), but card text can still compete with bright garden edges on OLED.

**Code limitation:**  
`DispatchQueue.main.asyncAfter(deadline: .now() + 1.5)` in `UnlockCelebrationView.onAppear`.

**Suggested improvement:**  
Allow immediate dismiss (tap outside or Continue always enabled). Use opaque card panel (`Theme.cardSurface`) for text block. Queue multiple unlocks with “2 of 3” indicator so users aren’t stuck in sequential full-screen flows.

---

## 20. Developer controls exposed in production Settings

**User experience & operation context:**  
A curious user opening Settings sees “Developer” with “Enable Bloom+ override.” Accidental tap flips entitlement locally; support/debug confusion if they think they subscribed.

**Code limitation:**  
`SettingsView` Developer section always visible.

**Suggested improvement:**  
`#if DEBUG` only, or hidden gesture for internal builds. Production: single “Restore purchases” under Bloom+.

## Summary

Sober’s core metaphor—a living garden tied to sobriety time—is strong and the Home spine is the right primary surface. The highest-impact UX work now is **making numbers and slips trustworthy**, **closing the loop on check-ins (reflection + vitality + placement)**, and **showing earned progress without hiding it behind Pro**. Secondary wins are **discovery** (Progress/collection), **off-app check-in** (widget/watch/notifications), and **visual craft** so the app feels as intentional as the recovery journey it supports.
