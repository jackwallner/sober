Sobriety Milestones: What 30, 60, 90 Days and 1 Year Mean

By Jack Wallner · Last updated October 15, 2026

Sobriety milestones are the day counts people mark on the way to a year: 24 hours, 30, 60 and 90 days, 6 and 9 months, then each anniversary. They are a custom, not a medical threshold. Alcoholics Anonymous does not issue the chips and coins itself, and each group decides its own schedule. Research does show measurable changes after about a month off alcohol, which is why the 30-day mark gets so much weight.

General information, not medical advice. Sober is a tracking tool and does not diagnose or treat anything. If you drink heavily, talk to a doctor before you stop, because sudden withdrawal can be dangerous.

The usual milestone ladder

There is no official schedule. Alcoholics Anonymous says its General Service Office does not make or sell chips, coins or medallions, and that because every group is autonomous, the customs for marking sobriety are a local choice. Colors and exact intervals differ from one meeting to the next. The ladder most people mean looks like this.

30 days: what a month off looks like in the research

The best-known evidence on one month of abstinence is a 2018 study in BMJ Open from London's Royal Free Hospital. It followed 94 moderate-to-heavy drinkers (average intake about 258 grams of alcohol a week, no known liver disease or alcohol dependence) who stopped for a month, and 47 who kept drinking. After a month, the abstaining group's median insulin resistance score was down 25.9%, systolic blood pressure down 6.6% and weight down 1.5%. The group that kept drinking showed no significant change.

It was an observational study of people who chose to stop, so it shows an association, not a guarantee. It also does not describe what happens to someone with alcohol dependence. Treat it as one data point on why the first month is worth marking.

60 days: the quiet middle

Nothing in the research makes day 60 a cutoff, and some groups skip the chip entirely. Its value is practical: the novelty of quitting has faded, and you are running on routine. If you want a reason to mark it, Sober's health timeline places a blood pressure marker near this point, citing American Heart Association guidance, and the 30-day study above also saw lower blood pressure. Individual results vary.

90 days: why it gets so much attention

Ninety days is a quarter of a year, which makes it an easy line to draw and a satisfying one to cross. It is also a natural point to reassess what is working: which routines, people and places help, and which ones do not. The number itself is a custom, not a clinical finish line.

1 year: what the number does and does not say

A year covers every holiday, birthday and bad week once. On the health side, the National Cancer Institute says stopping alcohol is associated with lower risks of oral cavity and esophageal cancers, and that it can take years for risks to return to those of never drinkers. Your own picture depends on how much you drank and your health history, so ask a doctor what a year means for you.

If you slip

The National Institute on Drug Abuse reports relapse rates of 40 to 60 percent for substance use disorders, similar to other chronic conditions, and says relapse does not mean treatment has failed. A slip resets a counter. It does not erase the weeks that came before it, and many people restart from that date and keep going. If a slip worries you, a doctor, a counselor or a peer group can help you adjust your plan.

Getting support

If you want help with drinking, SAMHSA's National Helpline at 1-800-662-HELP (4357) is free, confidential and open 24 hours a day, every day, in English and Spanish. It gives referrals to local treatment and support groups. If you are in crisis, call or text 988. In an emergency, such as seizures, hallucinations or confusion during withdrawal, call 911.

Frequently asked questions

What are the sobriety milestones?

The common marks are 24 hours, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days, 6 months, 9 months and 1 year, then every year after. They are a custom rather than a medical standard, and local groups differ on which ones they mark.

Does Alcoholics Anonymous give out sobriety coins?

Individual groups often do, but AA's General Service Office says it does not make or sell chips, coins or medallions. Because every group is autonomous, the practice is a local choice.

What changes after 30 days without alcohol?

A 2018 BMJ Open study of 94 moderate-to-heavy drinkers who stopped for a month found median drops of 25.9% in insulin resistance, 6.6% in systolic blood pressure and 1.5% in weight. It was observational, and results vary by person.

What if I relapse before a milestone?

NIDA reports relapse rates of 40 to 60 percent for substance use disorders and says relapse does not mean treatment has failed. Many people restart the count and keep going, and a doctor or counselor can help adjust the plan.

Is it safe to stop drinking suddenly?

Not always. The NHS warns that stopping suddenly can be very dangerous if you are dependent on alcohol, and withdrawal symptoms can begin 6 to 12 hours after the last drink. Talk to a doctor before you stop.
